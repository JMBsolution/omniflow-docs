Income from senior Korean project-finance lending. **Estimated net distribution yield 3.5–4.5% per annum, and the estimate rests on assumptions we could not source.** The previously published 8–9% target has been withdrawn.

## Why the Target Was Withdrawn

Re-deriving this tier from sourced inputs produced approximately 3.8% net, not 8–9%. Working backwards, an 8.5% net figure would require roughly 12.5% gross at the Korean asset level after withholding — and nothing in Korea prices there except mezzanine and junior project finance, which is the Tier 3 mandate. Publishing 8–9% for a senior lending strategy would have described Tier 3 risk at a Tier 2 label.

Two further corrections follow from the same work. The value-add equity sleeve has been removed from this tier: repositioning assets generate little distributable cash during the hold and their return is realised at exit, so they cannot back a product that pays a quarterly distribution. And the tier no longer describes its assets as "stabilised" or "near completion" — the Korean senior coupons that reach the high single digits are construction-phase bridge loans, a materially different risk.

## What It Is

The Korea Growth Plus Fund targets a moderately higher risk-return profile than Tier 1 by combining senior project finance (PF) lending with value-add asset acquisitions. The fund accepts moderate construction-phase exposure or operational improvement risk in exchange for higher target yields.

## Strategy

The fund deploys capital through two primary channels:

**Senior PF Loans.** First-lien senior loans to Korean development projects in the Seoul metropolitan area, secured by land mortgages, trust beneficiary rights, and project completion guarantees from Tier-1 Korean construction firms. Maturities are typically 2–4 years.

**Value-Add Acquisitions.** Direct or co-investment positions in assets that generate immediate yield while offering operational improvement potential — examples include logistics centers near Korean third-generation new town developments, data center pre-completion acquisitions, and resort/condominium operating assets with rebranding or renovation upside.

## Risks

Tier 2 carries greater risk than Tier 1. Additional risk factors include:

- **Construction completion risk** — project delays or cost overruns affect repayment timing

- **Counterparty risk on construction guarantor** — depends on the credit of the Tier-1 construction firm providing completion guarantee

- **Asset stabilization risk** — for value-add acquisitions, projected operational improvements may not be realized on schedule

- **Refinancing risk** — at maturity, project may require refinancing to fully repay senior debt

- All Tier 1 risks (tenant, market, currency, liquidity, smart contract)

A complete risk disclosure is provided in the product IM at subscription.
